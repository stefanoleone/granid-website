What the revised nDSG actually requires of law firms

28 May 2026 Granid 1 min read
Compliance Data sovereignty

The nDSG has been in force since September 2023. For law firms weighing AI tools, three of its principles decide whether a deployment is defensible.

The revised nDSG modernised Swiss data-protection law and aligned much of it with the GDPR. For a law firm, the practical question is narrow: under what conditions can client data be processed by an AI system at all?

Three principles that decide AI adoption

  • Purpose limitation. Data collected for a mandate may not be repurposed, including as training or telemetry for a third-party model.
  • Data minimisation. Only what the task needs should be processed, and no copy should persist beyond it.
  • Cross-border transfer. Sending personal data abroad requires an adequate level of protection. Cloud AI tools routinely move that data across borders without the firm seeing it happen.

An on-premise system satisfies all three by construction: the data never moves, is never repurposed, and never leaves the country.

Why architecture beats policy

A data-processing agreement is a promise. The nDSG cares about outcomes. When processing happens entirely on a Mac in the firm's own office, there is no transfer to assess, no sub-processor to vet, and no residual risk to disclose. Compliance stops being a document and becomes a property of the system.
