Your first hour with Legal Intelligence

4 September 2026 Granid 11 min read
Early Access

A step-by-step guide for Early Access firms: from a blank Mac to your first researched answer, reviewed contract stack, and drafted agreement.

Welcome to the Early Access Program. This post walks you through everything from installing Legal Intelligence to doing real work with it. It is written for the lawyer who will actually use the product day to day, not for an IT department. If you have half an hour and a coffee, you can be running your first legal research query by the end of it.

Along the way we show real screenshots of the application, so even colleagues who have not installed anything yet can see exactly how it looks and works.

What Legal Intelligence is

Legal Intelligence is an AI assistant for Swiss law firms that runs entirely inside your own office. It ingests your documents and answers questions about Swiss law with verifiable citations, drafts contracts, and reviews stacks of agreements at once. The important part: no data ever leaves your premises. The language model, the search, the legal corpus, and your client files all live on one machine in your office. Nothing is sent to a cloud provider, which is what makes it a fit for the confidentiality duties every Swiss lawyer already carries.

The shape of it: one server, every browser

Legal Intelligence is a client-server application, and understanding that makes everything else easy.

You install it once, on a single Apple Silicon Mac that sits in your office (a Mac mini is the typical choice, provisioned with enough unified memory to run the AI locally). That Mac is the server. It holds the AI models, the Swiss legal database, and all your firm's data.

Everyone else just opens a web browser. Each lawyer and assistant works from their own device, at their own desk, by visiting the address of the office Mac on the local network. There is nothing to install on those machines, and nothing to keep updated.

This is what makes Legal Intelligence a natural fit for a Windows-centric firm. The one Mac is an appliance in the server cupboard; your lawyers keep their Windows laptops, their existing setup, their habits. They simply open Chrome or Edge and log in. The AI runs on the Mac, the data stays on the Mac, and the Windows machines never touch anything sensitive: they are just windows onto the workspace.

In one line: the Mac is the brain and the vault; the browsers are the screens.

1. Installation

Installation happens once, on the office Mac. You download the Granid installer and run it. It first checks that the machine is ready (a recent macOS, Apple Silicon, enough memory, and enough free disk for the models and the legal database), then downloads the application and prepares everything. Every step reports as it goes, so you can watch it work.

When it finishes, the application is running as a background service on the Mac, and you simply open its address in a browser. That is the last time anyone needs to touch the Mac directly.

Later on, if you ever run the installer again, it recognises the existing installation and offers to reinstall (to update) or uninstall. The reassuring part is spelled out on screen: uninstalling removes only the software. Your firm's documents, the downloaded models, and the legal database all stay on disk, so a reinstall finds them again without downloading everything from scratch.

2. First-run setup

The very first time you open Legal Intelligence in a browser, a short setup wizard runs. It has a handful of steps and takes a few minutes.

License. You paste the license token Granid sent you by email. The wizard reads it and confirms your firm's name, plan, and validity. This is the only key the product needs.

AI processing. On a properly specced office Mac, the AI runs on that machine, and the models are downloaded once, now. This is a large, one-time download (the language model, the embedding model, and the reranker). The wizard shows progress and how much disk space is involved.

The wizard also checks the machine and is honest about the trade-offs. If you are just evaluating on a trial, or on a more modest Mac that cannot run the models locally, it says so and offers a secure cloud option so you can start right away on any hardware. The choice is spelled out, including what it means for your data: a paid on-premise installation keeps everything on the Mac and sends nothing outside your office, while the trial cloud mode processes your questions and document excerpts on a secure external service. You decide with the facts in front of you.

Legal database. Legal Intelligence then downloads the Swiss legal corpus onto the Mac: federal legislation, court decisions, and legal commentary. This is a large download, and the clever part is that it continues in the background. You can finish setup and start working while it fills up; legal research simply switches on the moment it is ready.

Logo and administrator. You can add your firm's logo (it appears in the header and on exported PDFs), and you create the first administrator account. On a single-seat plan the admin is also a full user; on a multi-seat plan the administrator then invites the rest of the firm.

3. Logging in

Legal Intelligence uses passwordless magic-link login. You enter your email, and a one-time login link is sent to you. There are no passwords to set, forget, or leak. Every user signs in the same way from their own browser.

4. The home dashboard

Once you are in, the home screen is built around your actual work. It greets you, shows the matters you opened most recently, lists your upcoming and overdue deadlines across every matter, and gives you a searchable table of the matters you are responsible for. The left sidebar is your firm's client list and workspace; the footer carries your plan and the language switcher.

5. Clients and matters

Your work is organised the way a firm already thinks: clients, and matters within each client.

The Clients workspace gives you a firm-wide overview: how many clients and matters you have, deadline load, a twelve-month acquisition trend, a breakdown by city, and outcomes, followed by the full client directory.

Creating a client is a single short form.

Open a client and you get a portfolio workspace: the client's contact details, a client-wide AI assistant in the centre, and a right-hand rail with deadlines, the client's matters, and files.

Open a matter and you get the matter workbench: the assistant takes the centre of the screen, and the right rail carries the matter's deadlines, its document reviews, and its files. This one screen is where most of the day happens.

6. Legal research

This is the heart of the product. Ask a question about Swiss law in plain language, in German, French, Italian, or English.

Legal Intelligence does not just paste back a paragraph. It plans the research, breaks your question into sub-questions, and searches legislation, court decisions, and commentary for each one. The step trail shows you exactly what it did, so the reasoning is transparent rather than a black box.

The answer arrives with its sources attached: the exact statutory articles, the court decisions that cite them (with official deep links you can open), and the relevant commentary with its attribution. Every citation carries a verification mark, because the system checks each one against the source rather than trusting the model's word for it. This is the difference between an AI that sounds confident and one a lawyer can actually rely on.

7. Bringing in your own documents

Research over Swiss law is only half the story. You also want answers about your files. Upload documents into a matter (PDF, Word, scans) and Legal Intelligence reads them, runs OCR where needed, and indexes them locally so the assistant can search and cite them. As with everything else, the files never leave the Mac.

8. Reviewing a stack of contracts at once

When you have several contracts to compare (a pile of NDAs, a set of leases, a batch of employment agreements), Tabular Review turns them into a spreadsheet. You define the columns you care about (the parties, the term, the penalty clause, the governing law), and Legal Intelligence fills in a row for every document at once.

Every extracted value is grounded. Click any cell and it shows you the exact quote and page it came from, so you can verify the answer in seconds instead of re-reading the contract.

9. Drafting a contract

Draft Studio turns a plain-language brief into a working document. Describe what you need (here, a one-way NDA under Swiss law with a specific penalty clause and term) and choose the language.

Legal Intelligence produces a full, structured draft on the right, ready to read and refine.

The refinement is where it earns its place. Ask for a change in plain language ("make the term five years, raise the penalty") and the edit comes back as tracked changes, exactly as a colleague's redline would, so you can see precisely what moved before you accept it. When you are happy, publish it to PDF into the matter.

10. Getting help

There is an in-app Help assistant on every screen (the button in the bottom-right corner). Ask it how to do something and it answers from the product manual. It is the fastest way to learn a feature without leaving your work.

You will also see occasional product tips that point out capabilities you might have missed.

One product, four languages

Switzerland is trilingual, and so is Legal Intelligence. The entire interface, and the assistant, speak German, French, Italian, and English. Each person picks their own language from the footer; the same firm, the same data, in whichever language each lawyer prefers.

That is your first hour

From a blank Mac you now have a running installation, your clients and matters, researched answers with verifiable citations, a reviewed stack of contracts, and a drafted agreement, all without a single byte leaving your office.
