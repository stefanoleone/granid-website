Refer to the product as **`Legal Intelligence`** (the brand), not "the Legal Intelligence app" or "the Legal Intelligence stack" or "L'application Legal Intelligence" / "L'app Legal Intelligence" / "Die Legal-Intelligence-App". Drop the generic noun and let the brand stand alone.

**Why:** Stefano flagged this on 2026-05-07 reviewing the GWEB-18 hardware-page rewrite. Reasoning: the product name is `Legal Intelligence`. Wrapping it in "app" or "stack" demotes the brand into a generic category. Lawyers should read the brand as a proper noun, like Vontobel or Stripe — those companies don't call themselves "the Vontobel app".

**How to apply:**
- Sentences like "The Legal Intelligence app reads from indexes" become "Legal Intelligence reads from indexes"
- "Die Legal-Intelligence-App liest …" becomes "Legal Intelligence liest …"
- "L'application Legal Intelligence lit …" becomes "Legal Intelligence lit …"
- "L'app Legal Intelligence legge …" becomes "Legal Intelligence legge …"
- Same for "Legal Intelligence stack" / "Legal-Intelligence-Stack" / "stack Legal Intelligence" — drop "stack", use the brand alone.
- Acceptable contexts where the wrapper noun stays: a glossary line ("Legal Intelligence is the on-premise app for Swiss law firms") where you're explicitly defining the category once, not in running prose.
- Same rule applies to the future products: `Meeting Intelligence`, `Accounting Intelligence`, `Compliance Intelligence` — never "the Meeting Intelligence app", just the brand name.
