General-purpose models invent case law and misquote statutes with complete
fluency. In a legal context that is not a quirk. It is a liability. Granid
treats every citation as a claim that must be checked.

## Answers grounded in your own documents

Granid answers from your documents and the Swiss legal corpus, not from what the
model thinks it remembers. Each reference is resolved back to the source text and
the quoted passage is matched against what the provision actually says.

> If a citation cannot be verified against source material, it does not appear in the answer.

## What you see

- The reference, with a link to the exact passage it rests on.
- The surrounding context, so you can confirm the reading yourself.
- Nothing the system could not substantiate.

This is the **Human in the Loop** principle applied to the highest-risk part of
legal AI: the lawyer always sees the ground truth behind every claim.
